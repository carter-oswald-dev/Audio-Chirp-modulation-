HOW THIS WORKS

Text is converted to 5-bit Baudot code, then each 5-bit symbol is encoded as a cyclically time-shifted chirp (a linear frequency sweep) — the same family of technique LoRa radios and GPS use to pull signals up out of the noise. Every transmission opens with a short header, always sent at a fixed 5 bps regardless of the payload's own rate, that tells the decoder exactly what data rate, error-correction level, checksum setting, and repeat count follow — so decoding never requires knowing or guessing the sender's settings in advance. A distinct down-sweep marks the start of the header and of each payload repeat, so the decoder can find its place even with an arbitrary recording start time. Decoding happens entirely in your browser: nothing you upload leaves your device.

Data rate trades speed for range: a slower symbol rate spends more time per bit, which buys more processing gain against noise. Error correction repeats each symbol multiple times and decodes by majority vote, trading transmission time for robustness against dropped or corrupted symbols. CRC32 adds a short checksum so the decoder can tell you whether the recovered text is likely correct, rather than silently returning corrupted text.

The distance-based rate suggestion is a rough physical estimate (spreading loss + assumed ambient noise), not a guarantee — real outdoor conditions (wind, other noise sources, mic placement) vary considerably. Treat it as a starting point to test from, not a promise.
